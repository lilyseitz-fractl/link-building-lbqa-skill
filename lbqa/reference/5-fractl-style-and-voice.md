# 5 — Fractl Style, AP Rules, Sourcing & Voice (Link-Building)

The rules every link-building write-up follows. **The client's CM doc overrides anything here** when it conflicts — always apply the CM doc on top of these defaults.

---

## Voice & point of view

- **The study speaks in first person: "we / our."** "We surveyed 1,003 Americans," "our data found," "we tested four AI tools." The write-up is published on the client's behalf, so the research is "ours."
- **Name the client in third person where it reads naturally**, especially in the intro and the About section. "Payless Power surveyed 1,003 Americans…", "ZipHealth commissioned a survey of 1,002 U.S. adults…". Both can appear in the same piece: name the client once to establish who ran the study, then carry on with "we/our."
- **Use "you" for reader-application moments** — when you turn from the data to what it means for the reader ("If you're deciding whether to upgrade or replace, the gap is now about $6,600 a year").
- **Never use "the reader," "the user," "the consumer," "the customer"** as a stand-in for the audience. Use "you" or name the group ("drivers," "singles," "fans").
- **Conversational but authoritative.** Active voice. Direct sentences that lead with subject and verb.

## AP style essentials (the ones that come up most)

- **Numbers:** spell out zero through nine; use numerals for 10 and up.
- **Never begin a sentence with a numeral** — including percentages. Reword so the number isn't first, or spell it out. "Forty-three percent of professionals…" (sentence start) but "…and 43% said…" (mid-sentence).
- **Percent:** the examples spell out "percent" at the start of a sentence and use the **% symbol** mid-sentence ("88% of professionals"). Follow that pattern unless the CM doc says otherwise.
- **Oxford/serial comma:** always ("apples, bananas, and oranges").
- **Periods and commas go inside quotation marks.**
- **Quotation marks/apostrophes** consistent throughout (all curly or all straight).
- **One space between sentences.**
- **"US" in titles** (no periods); **"U.S." in headers and body** (with periods).
- **Past tense for all findings/stats** — the study was conducted in the past. "Most respondents said…," "ChatGPT caught 85% of contradictions." Present tense only for general truisms.
- **Avoid absolute language** ("will," "must," "all," "every," "always"). Prefer "may," "can," "could," "most," "some." Clients flag absolutes repeatedly.
- **Title case for headings and subheadings** by default; **FAQ questions in sentence case** (they're full sentences). CM doc overrides the case rule.
- **No trailing periods on headings** (question marks are fine for question headings).

## Sourcing rules (link-building specific — stricter than onsite)

- **External links only when genuinely necessary.** In a link-building WU, add an external link only to: cite another study, reference a recent event, define an unfamiliar term, or back a claim that needs support. Don't pepper the piece with external links.
- **Domain Authority (DA) 70+** for every external source in a link-building WU. (DA doesn't apply to internal links.)
- **Primary sources only.** Link the original publisher/authority that collected or reported the data — not a roundup, aggregator, or site summarizing someone else's data.
- **Publication date within 2 years** (1 year or less preferred).
- **No paywalled sources** (this explicitly includes The New York Times, Fortune, and The Wall Street Journal).
- **Never link a client competitor**, and never link a page whose main purpose is selling a product/service. Also avoid pages that *mention* competitors. When unsure whether a site is a competitor, flag it for the DJ rather than linking.
- **Never use Wikipedia, Reddit, Quora, or other UGC** to support a factual claim. Exception: UGC may be linked/quoted when the user-generated content *itself* is the subject (sentiment, trends) and the source is relevant — frame the context clearly.
- **The client's own study data is not externally cited** — it's "our" data and lives in the asset/findings, sourced as "Source: [Client] Study."
- **Strip tracking parameters** from every URL (`utm_*`, etc.). Use the clean base URL. (AI tools often append these — always check.)
- **Don't plagiarize.** No string of more than 3–4 words copied from any source; paraphrase and link.

## Anchor text

- Descriptive, concise, relevant to the destination page. The words around the link matter — write naturally.
- **No "click here," "read more," "learn more."** The anchor itself is the call.
- Don't chain multiple links back-to-back.
- Don't link the same URL/keyword repeatedly unless it genuinely helps the reader.

## Structure conventions

- **Every heading has at least one sentence of text under it** before any list or image. Don't drop a heading straight onto a bullet list or image.
- **Introduce every bulleted/numbered list** with a lead-in sentence ending in a colon. Use a conversational lead-in, not a bare label.
  - ✅ "Here's where the cost gap shows up:" ❌ "Cost gap:"
  - **Never leave the PC's "Study Findings:" label as the lead-in** — that's an internal PC tag, not reader-facing copy.
- **Capitalize the first letter of each bullet.** End a bullet with a period if it's a complete sentence or has a bold lead-in; no period on one-word bullets.
- **Bullet lead-ins:** when a bullet starts with a short lead-in phrase, bold it and follow with a period, then the sentence. Be consistent within a piece.
- **Only use a bulleted list for three or more items.**
- **Avoid "and more" at the end of a list** — fold that meaning into the lead-in.
- **Tables** are in-text elements (not designed assets). Sentence case all cells, column labels, and row labels (title row can follow client heading case). Bold the title row and the row/column labels; use thin borders.

## Methodology, About, Fair Use (always present)

- **Methodology:** carry over the PC's methodology accurately. Include the rounding disclaimer if any percentages don't total 100% ("Percentages not totaling 100% are due to rounding"). For surveys, state that participants were adults 18 or older, plus sample size, fielding period, and demographic breakdown. Don't invent methodology detail that isn't in the PC.
- **About [Client]:** 2–3 sentences on the company, tied to the study topic where possible. May include an internal link and a CTA. Check the CM doc — some clients supply a boilerplate to paste; some skip this section.
- **Fair Use Statement:** 1–2 sentences granting noncommercial reuse with attribution and a link back to the client.

## What does NOT belong in a link-building WU

- **No CTA for the client's product/service in the conclusion.** (A CTA can live in the About section.)
- **No internal process/meta language** (no "Asana," "the editor," "client-approved," "Study Findings:" label, "per the PC").
- **No phone numbers** unless the DJ explicitly provided one to include.
